# Welcome to the SUCCESS Toolkit

Urinary catheters are commonly used during and after surgery, but they can cause infections, injuries, and other complications. The **Surgical Urinary Catheter Care Enhancement Safety Study (SUCCESS)** developed practical, evidence-informed resources to help surgical teams improve catheter safety.

Led by **Dr. Jennifer Meddings**, SUCCESS was funded by the **Agency for Healthcare Research and Quality (AHRQ)** and developed in partnership with the **Michigan Surgical Quality Collaborative (MSQC)**. The project brought together researchers, surgeons, nurses, and participating hospitals to improve perioperative urinary catheter care.

This toolkit supports appropriate catheter use, consistent management of postoperative urinary retention, and safer catheter insertion and removal. It includes clinical guides and algorithms, implementation materials, educational resources, patient-facing tools, and videos that healthcare teams can adapt to their needs.

## Toolkit Implementation
Better tools are needed to measure and reduce perioperative urinary catheter use, as well as manage postoperative urinary retention and urinary catheter-associated complications.  A comprehensive toolkit was developed, implemented, and evaluated in collaboration with a pilot group of [Michigan Surgical Quality Collaborative (MSQC)](https://msqc.org/) hospitals’, surgeons, and nurses. The novel measures and toolkit could dramatically reduce common, costly, and morbid complications related to perioperative urinary catheter use, adaptable to many other surgical procedures, to improve the safety of our most vulnerable patients.

The toolkit intervention will address clinician knowledge and urinary catheterization skills, as well as communication and implementation challenges anticipated to impact catheter use in different types of perioperative clinical settings. This intervention aims to reduce inappropriate perioperative urinary catheter use, catheter-associated complications including infections and catheter-associated trauma, and improve the management of postoperative urinary retention.
